# Content quality, E-E-A-T and content briefs

## Who / How / Why test (Google's own heuristic)

- **Who** made it - is the author or business clear and credible?
- **How** was it made - is the method or first-hand experience shown?
- **Why** does it exist - to help people, or mainly to rank?

## E-E-A-T signals

- **Experience**: first-hand photos, original data, "we tested", specific details only a user would know.
- **Expertise**: named author with relevant credentials, accurate and deep coverage.
- **Authoritativeness**: mentions and links from respected sources in the field, consistent brand presence.
- **Trust** (the most important): contact details, about page, policies, correct facts, sources cited, dates shown and updated, HTTPS, honest reviews.

## Quality gates (minimum words as a thin-content signal, not a target)

| Page type | Min words | Unique content |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | 500 | 100% |
| Service / feature | 800 | 100% |
| Blog post | 1,500 | 100% |
| Product | 400 | 80%+ |
| Category | 400 | unique intro, not only product tiles |
| Location (primary / secondary) | 600 / 500 | 60%+ / 40%+ |
| Landing page | 600 | 100% |

**Location pages**: warn at 30+, stop and ask for justification at 50+. City-name-swapped pages are doorway pages. Mass AI-generated pages with no unique value are "scaled content abuse" in Google's spam policies.

## On-page basics

- Title 30-60 characters (Google truncates by pixel width), primary topic near the start, brand at the end, unique per page.
- Meta description ~120-160 characters, specific, matches the page; unique.
- One H1; logical H2/H3; question-shaped headings where people ask questions.
- Internal links: descriptive anchors to and from related pages; key pages linked from the homepage or navigation.
- External links to sources for claims.
- Readability: short paragraphs, plain words, front-loaded answers.

## Content brief (for a writer or for Claude)

1. Mode: new page or refresh of an existing URL.
2. SERP check: read the top 5-10 results for the main query; note intent (informational, commercial, transactional, navigational), page type and format that rank.
3. Brief: target query + 3-8 related queries, intent, page type, title and meta draft, H2/H3 outline, questions to answer, internal links to add (from and to), entities and facts to include with sources, and **information gain**: what this page will say that the top results do not (original data, a real example, an opinion backed by experience). No information gain = no brief; say so.
4. The site's own language and market; the brief must fit the site's real offering.
