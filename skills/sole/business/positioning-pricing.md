# Positioning and pricing

## Positioning (fill in before writing any marketing)

1. **Competitive alternatives**: what customers would use if you did not exist.
2. **Unique attributes**: what you have that those alternatives lack.
3. **Value**: what those attributes let the customer do (with proof).
4. **Best-fit customers**: who cares most about that value.
5. **Market category**: the frame that makes your value obvious.

One-line output: "For [best-fit customer] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [key value]. Unlike [alternative], we [differentiator]."

Test it: would a competitor's name fit the same sentence? Then it is not positioning yet.

## Pricing

- **Three axes**: packaging (what is in each tier), value metric (what you charge per), price level.
- **Value metric** grows with the customer's value: seats, usage, contacts, projects, revenue. Avoid metrics customers cannot predict.
- **Good-better-best**: three tiers, middle one is the target, top one anchors. Differentiate by value, not by random feature removal.
- **Starting price**: pick a price you can learn from; most early products are under-priced. Avoid $9 for B2B.
- **Research**: Van Westendorp (too cheap / cheap / expensive / too expensive) or simple willingness-to-pay interviews; check competitor prices with `business/competitor.md`.
- **Raising prices**: signs are high conversion, no price objections, more value shipped. Grandfather or give notice to existing customers.
